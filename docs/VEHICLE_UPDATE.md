## State synchronization and update order

`0x009bd7f0` receives the verified general car's body. Instructions explicitly load body `+0xdb0` before calling the linked linear/angular/pose getters, storing results at body `+0x15c0`, `+0x15d0` and `+0x15e0`. It also handles small-value cleanup, parameter/input copies and mass/inertia synchronization. This explains why body values are a synchronized view rather than necessarily the physics solver's only authoritative storage. [Linked-state observations](VEHICLE_STATE.md) remain non-atomic snapshots.

The registered car-job method `0x00a0a290` reads body pointer at job `+4` and f32 delta at job `+8`, invokes synchronization, calls `0x00a06a90` with that delta, then calls `0x00a03220` and signals completion through its embedded job object. The synchronous wrapper `0x00a09a80` follows a similar sequence and explicitly publishes pose/velocity to linked state. Table `0x00d15438` contains the job method at its second entry; an adjacent source string identifies CarJob. A raw aligned-word search found that method pointer; surrounding words are a mixture of floats and a method table, not one homogeneous table. Earlier neighboring-address xref guesses therefore did not identify its construction.

`0x00a06a90` invokes body update `0x00a03670` only when the supplied delta is positive. The latter saves recent deltas in a five-element ring and prior velocity vectors, then calls drivetrain, assist, wheel/contact and other consumers. Verified instruction references place these calls in order:

- `0x00a00e10`, then `0x009dfc50`: embedded dynamics and further wheel/contact handling.
- Later consumers include perturbation `0x009b44b0`, wheel smoothing/spin storage and `0x009b9500` orientation assistance.

Do not translate Ghidra's spurious float10 return/stack variables directly into Rust. Instruction inspection is required to recover real arguments and operation order. The initial wheel-vector caller `0x00a053f0` proved to be a sound-related path, and `min_time_step` / `locked_timestep` string references led to countdown/slow-motion/result configuration. ## Embedded dynamics context and substeps

At call `0x00a00f20`, instructions supply **body +0x3d0** as the receiver for `0x009f1e10`. Its context `+0x5d4` is a body back-pointer (effective body `+0x9a4`), and context `+0x70c` gates a drivetrain-specific branch. Four local wheel blocks use stride `0x170`; the main body's wheel records remain the separately verified `0x150` stride.

The outer routine prepares data through `0x009e41f0`, iterates `0x009c1f60`, then finalizes through `0x009e4b10`. The substep kernel handles wheel/coupling variables and invokes further helpers. Its complete force/contact contract is not implemented.

Original instructions establish this supported scheduling contract:

1. Multiply incoming f32 delta by stored f32 word **`4479ffff` = 999.9999389648438**.
2. Convert with CVTTSS2SI (truncate toward zero) and enforce at least one substep.
3. Compute f32 `inverse_count = 1 / count`, then `sub_delta = inverse_count * delta`.
4. Start an interpolation weight at f32 1. Call the kernel with `(sub_delta, weight)`, subtract `inverse_count` in f32 after each call, and repeat exactly `count` times.

This is delta-dependent internal substepping, not proof of a fixed 1000 Hz game tick. For stored delta `3c23d70a` (approximately .01), the controlled original executes 9 substeps. Delta `3c888889` (approximately 1/60) gives 16; `.25` gives 249. Adjacent f32 values around .002/.016/.017 can change the count, as retained boundary cases demonstrate. The job's incoming delta source, paused/slow-motion behavior, external solver cadence and interactions with worker scheduling require runtime measurement.

After finalization, context `+0x620` and `+0x630` contribute to body velocity. In each of four lanes, with separate f32 operations:

```
linear  += delta * linear_rate
angular += delta * angular_rate
position += (delta * linear_rate) * (delta * .5)
```

The position term is only this routine's post-solver correction. It is not the full `position += velocity * delta` integration, and no rotation integration is established here.

Preparation `0x009e41f0` establishes the rates before the substep loop: it compares current velocities with the saved pre-update body vectors at `+0xb20` and `+0xb30`, stores `(current - previous) * (1 / delta)` at context `+0x620` / `+0x630`, and restores previous velocity to the body. The outer additions therefore preserve contributions made before the wheel solver. They are not simply finalizer force outputs. Preparation also computes per-wheel rate terms and resets the context clock. Private evidence: `local/behavior/20261007T0157123844953Z/9e41f0.c.txt`.

Finalization `0x009e4b10` combines wheel axes and accumulated impulse scalars, subtracts body position to form a lever arm, obtains angular response through `0x009cc3d0`, and adds impulse divided by mass to linear velocity. Helper `0x009cc6e0` combines these same angular/linear additions. `0x00a15580` is a 0x80-byte state copy with its receiver supplied separately; decompiler output that omits that receiver must not be interpreted as inertia preparation. Finalizer branches can replace inertia components temporarily. Their activation and complete wheel-force inputs remain unresolved. Evidence: `20261007T0157123844953Z/9e4b10.c.txt` and `20261007T0200403232492Z/` under `local/behavior/`.

The bump ratio is displacement divided by a travel denominator, capped above at one; only positive ratios produce the twelfth-power bump term. ## Conditional four-wheel response preparation (F069)

`grid-world::wheel_coupling::prepare` independently reconstructs the extra-axes-inactive branch of `0x009e3970`. Inputs are an initial body state and four supplied world points, impulse axes (context +30) and secondary projection axes (+40). First record each point velocity projected on its impulse axis. For each initiating wheel, apply a test impulse `axis * (mass * f32(38d1b717))` to a fresh initial state, then compute the projected velocity change at all four receiving wheels and multiply by the reciprocal test strength. This forms the 4×4 normal response matrix, rows initiating wheels, columns receiving wheels.

The secondary matrix removes the receiving impulse-axis component from that velocity change, projects the remainder onto the initiating secondary axis, and divides by the dot product between initiating secondary and receiving impulse axes. It uses the observed SSE reciprocal seed and refinement `(1 - denominator*seed)*(seed*numerator) + seed*numerator`, retaining cancellation/rounding. Singular/nonfinite projections explicitly fail in the independent diagnostic. Point/axis construction, temporary pose prediction and active extra axes are outside this contract.
