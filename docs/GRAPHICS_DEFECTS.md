## Rotating wheels / lettering aliasing

Owner acceptance requirement (16 October): wheels must remain visually smooth at 30 through 300 FPS. Validate identical physical wheel speeds at 30, 60, 144 and 300 FPS, including acceleration, braking, reverse and standstill. Obtain angular velocity from the physical state, never from subtraction of wrapped rendered angles (multiple revolutions can be lost). Define exposure in seconds and distinguish an authored fixed shutter from frame-relative shutter; do not silently make blur depend on the number of rendered frames. Inspect presentation interpolation separately: the current physics step is 0.016s, so render frames above 62.5Hz can repeat wheel poses even with correct physics. Preserve wheel physics, steering, suspension and real rim geometry. This is a requirement, not a completed validation.

Owner reports jerky tyre lettering and spokes at speed. Treat this separately from whole-car history trails and fixed-step presentation interpolation. Compare angular speed, displayed frame interval and shutter interval; keep actual physical wheel phase/steering unchanged. Evaluate rotational shutter sampling/material filtering with a smooth transition at low speed, preserving real3D rim silhouette and occlusion.

Primary research: [Shawn Hargreaves, Detail Texture Motion Blur](https://shawnhargreaves.com/detail_blur/detail_blur.html) explains time-domain aliasing in racing textures, integration over an exposure interval, preprocessing/filtering options and his implementation in MotoGP2. This supports the temporal-filtering approach, not an assertion about Forza wheel internals. [McGuire et al., A Reconstruction Filter for Plausible Motion Blur](https://www.casual-effects.com/research/McGuire2012Blur/McGuire12Blur.pdf) is a primary reconstruction reference. Bevy's pinned local TAA shader/components are the implementation reference for this prototype; the [official0.14 motion-vector discussion](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-14/) identifies prior/current animated vertex data as relevant. No specific Forza Horizon wheel algorithm verified yet; forum anecdotes are not implementation evidence. GT Racing2 GDC2014 slides found via search discuss sun lightshaft radial blur, not wheel blur, and must not be cited as wheel evidence. GT7 presentation URL could not be opened; no findings inferred from it.
