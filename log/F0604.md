F604 Android pad rumble and phone vibration from the GameFFB task (2026-10-27, Claude Code; coordinator task: port force feedback and rumble, backlog row 22, step 3 after F598 and F602).
- Doc: [docs/FORCE_FEEDBACK.md](../docs/FORCE_FEEDBACK.md) §7.2; [docs/ANDROID.md](../docs/ANDROID.md) "Controls" (rumble is no longer listed as not covered). AUTHORED throughout: the original has no Android version.
- What vibrates: the original's `XBOX360 Controller` device (delay 0.1 s) runs the same GameFFB task as on Windows. Its two motor channels carry only effect samples (surfaces and rumble strips, gear changes, collisions, landings, skids, a damaged wheel); the steady force and friction never reach motors (`B8CD80`), so a plain corner does not buzz.
- `crates/grid-app/src/force_feedback/android.rs`: the "FFB updateTask" thread (`Sleep(10)`, output, task), attached to the VM once (`android::attach_thread`). `force_feedback::Vibration` (pure, tested) turns the motor speeds into 8-bit levels and limits the JNI calls: a change at most every 30 ms, a stop at once, a running level renewed every 60 ms.
- `android.rs` `rumble(left, right)` → `MainActivity.rumble` → `Rumble.java` on the UI thread: a connected pad with vibrators (`InputDevice.getVibratorManager()`, API 31) gets left on vibrator 0 and right on vibrator 1 (one vibrator: the larger); a pad without vibrators gets nothing (the phone is then probably on a stand); no pad: the phone vibrator plays the larger level (amplitude control if present, else on from 128). 100 ms one-shots; `PadBridge.first()` names the pad. Manifest: `android.permission.VIBRATE` (normal permission).
- `crates/grid-app/src/ffb.rs` now builds for Windows and Android (no wheel on Android); `main.rs` installs it on both.
- Settings: `[input] force_feedback = off` stops it; `feedback_effects` 0..10 scales it (FORCE_FEEDBACK §7.1).
