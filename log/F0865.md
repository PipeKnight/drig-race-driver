Find what `r_encode` (2.5 to 5.7 ms, the top stage in every cell below 100 fps) costs per draw, explain Donington's "1280 slower than 2560", and cut the cost through a shared path with no visible change. Details: docs/PERFORMANCE.md, "Transparent draws: bindless car reflection overlay (F865)".

- Findings:
  - `r_encode` does not depend on resolution (Donington 1280 and 2560 baseline both 3.84 ms on the idle PC). The "1280 slower" was background load (F863). No LOD or culling choice depends on it.
  - Before: derby 97 transparent draws per frame, Donington 232, about one per item, because neighbouring overlays of different parts have different materials.
  - Grouping probe (not committed; it reorders overlays, so it changes the image): derby 38 draws, `r_encode` -0.11 ms; Donington 175, -0.15 ms. About 2 to 3 µs per draw.
- Change: `CarReflectionOverlay` is `#[bindless]`. All overlay materials share one bind group and the shader indexes its textures by the mesh's material slot, so Bevy's batch-set merge joins consecutive overlays of different parts without reordering. Constants are `#[data(0, OverlayUniform, binding_array(10))]`: bindless `uniform` binds an array of uniform buffers, which drew wrong overlays on 8 to 28 % of pixels in the first build. Without binding arrays the material takes the old bind group and the shader's `#else` path.
- New diagnostic: `GRID_NO_BINDLESS=1` disables TEXTURE_BINDING_ARRAY and BUFFER_BINDING_ARRAY at device creation, to check that fallback on this PC.
- Image check (local/ab/crops-bl.ps1, bl2.ps1; HEAD local/ab/og.exe 0df648c09fa741b7 against local/ab/bl.exe bf90b61354da7171; 1280x720 fixed step): Donington 600 and derby 1500, baseline and Enhanced, touge 900.
