F729 Cars and camera drawn between fixed ticks: no motion judder above 62.5 FPS (2026-11-04, Claude Code).
- Coordinator task (owner, prototype e66e1c24963c): "игра показывает, словно я играю в 80-100 фпс, но картинка рваная, словно я играю в 20-30 фпс". Backlog row 116; details in docs/FRAME_STEPPING.md section 6.
- Cause [observed in code and in a window trace]:
  - The simulation steps in 16 ms ticks (62.5 Hz).
  - Every frame drew the last tick's pose: the cars, the opponents, the mirror, the drivers, the damage parts, and the chase camera (whose `Clock::Tick` also steps per tick).
  - Above 62.5 FPS, a frame without a tick repeated the picture, and the next frame moved a whole tick.
  - The original steps its world once per frame by the real frame time (docs/FRAME_STEPPING.md section 1), so its motion moves every frame.
- Fix, shared path for desktop and Android (main.rs, cockpit.rs, exterior_driver.rs, damage_parts.rs):
  - Render interpolation, one tick behind. `drive_tick` keeps every car's pose before the tick (`ShownPoses`). `shown_alpha` reads `Time<Fixed>::overstep_fraction()` after the fixed loop. `Driving::shown_pose` lerps and slerps between the two poses.
  - The camera's shot is blended between the last two ticks' shots (`ShownShots`).
  - These snap instead of blending: a move of more than 20 m, a view change, a stopped simulation.
  - Only interactive windows interpolate. Captures, headless runs and tests draw the last tick exactly as before.
  - The simulation is unchanged.
- New switches:
  - `GRID_TICK_INTERPOLATION=0` gives the old drawing;
  - `GRID_PRESENT_TRACE=1` prints a `present_frame` line per drawn frame (frame time, tick, alpha, the car and eye positions).
- Evidence. Washington, 11 opponents, autopilot, a real muted window. The same exe 4958d9bebb25, with the switch alternated over two rounds. Private: `local/judder/` (`wa-*.log`, `wb-*.log`, `final.log`, `steps.py`, plot `steps-wa.svg`).
  - The window ran at 128 FPS: mean frame 7.8 ms, p95 8.6 ms.
  - Measure: per drawn frame at speed, the car's step divided by its expected step.

    | Round | sd, off → on | frames not moving | frames over 1.75× | car–eye offset change per frame, sd |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | wa | 1.026 → 0.081 | 49.8 % → 0.5 % | 1075 → 2 | 1.45 → 0.89 cm |
    | wb | 1.008 → 0.081 | 48.6 % → 0.5 % | 1019 → 1 | 1.45 → 0.87 cm |

  - The eye gives the same figures. An earlier pair at about 55 FPS gave sd 0.317 → 0.083.
  - The first camera version oscillated between two shots after the sim stopped (scripted end). This was fixed with `Driving::frozen`, before the runs above.
- How it should look: motion moves on every frame at any frame rate, instead of a regular stutter whenever the FPS is not a multiple of 62.5. The picture is 16 ms behind the simulation.
- Not changed: the present mode (the player's choice in the video menu; AutoNoVsync can tear, which is a separate artefact). Wheel spin already used the overstep. The HUD, sound and lean read the tick.
- Tests: `cars_are_drawn_between_their_last_two_ticks`, `the_camera_is_drawn_between_the_shots_of_the_last_two_ticks`.
- Confirmation with the pushed source: release exe dfbbde081af7 in a window, interpolation on by default, `local/judder/final.log`. The window ran at about 85 FPS (mean frame 11.7 ms): sd 0.085, 0.6 % of frames not moving, none over 1.75×.
