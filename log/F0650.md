- Original [D] (F627, docs/COCKPIT.md "Steering wheel"): the human driver's wheel follows −(+0xE30) with no smoothing (0x85F8F0 → 0x858C20 → 0x851730). CAMERA_VIEWS §4 item 8 (a capture) is still open. The change is recorded as a deliberate deviation the owner requested.
- Port, general rather than keyboard-specific: `cockpit::steering` recovers the centre steering angle from the two front wheels' applied (cos, sin) at wheel +0x20/+0x24, the pair the renderer turns, with toe and rotation offset taken off. The geometry in `steering_coefficients::update` gives each wheel cot(a) ∓ track / (2 · wheelbase), so tan a = 2 t₁t₂ / (t₁ + t₂). Then s = −a / +0xE54, clamped to ±1, or −(+0xE30) without a lock. The Steering clip and the driver's steering poses and hand targets both use this s.
- Test [V] `cockpit::tests::lacetti_wheel_follows_the_front_wheels` (ignored, GRID_GAME_ROOT), on every 16 ms tick: the centre angle s · E54 lies between the two front wheels' angles. Series in worktree camera-dynamics-11a55d `local/cam/row94_test*.txt`.
  - Keyboard (assisted): a 0.05 s tap, release, a 1 s hold, release. s rises to full lock in about 0.12 s and returns to 0 in about 0.15 s, at most 0.145 per tick, the front wheels' own slew. Before, it jumped 0 → ±1 in one tick.
  - Wheel device (unassisted): a ramp from full left to full right; s equals −(+0xE30) within 2e-3 every tick, so a wheel still tracks 1:1.
  - Pad stick (the same assisted class as the keyboard), held at half at a standstill: s = 0.479 against a raw 0.5. The assist's response shaping makes the 4% difference, and at speed its speed scale narrows the front wheels and the cockpit wheel together. So a pad tracks the front wheels, close to but not exactly 1:1 with the stick.
