- New `crates/grid-app/src/cpu_place.rs`, `perf_test` words (Android and Linux; no effect elsewhere):
  - `pin_main_big`, `pin_main_prime`, `pin_render_big`, `pin_render_prime`, `pin_pool_big`, `pin_pool_noprime`: `sched_setaffinity` on our own threads. Core classes from `cpu_capacity` (fallback `cpuinfo_max_freq`).
  - `uclamp`: `sched_setattr` minimum utilisation clamp 512 on the main and render threads.
  - ADPF: the phone GPU session landed its own `adpf` word (`adpf.rs`, F934, with GPU-time reports) while this batch was waiting for the gate. On the rebase this batch dropped its duplicate ADPF session from `cpu_place.rs` and kept that one.
  - Always-on counters in `frame_stats` on Android and Linux: `cpu_<main|render|pool>_run` and `_wait` (ms per frame from `/proc/self/task/<tid>/schedstat`) and the share of frames that start on a little or the prime core.
  - Pool threads are registered through `on_thread_spawn` in mobile.rs. Four unit tests: core classes of a 1+4+3 CPU and of equal or unknown capacities, word parsing, the schedstat line.
- Heat (coordinator's 15-minute phone soak: throttling after about 2 min, 32 → 25 FPS) — new `crates/grid-app/src/thermal.rs`, docs/ANDROID_CPU.md section 4:
  - Always on under Android: a `grid-thermal` thread reads `AThermal_getThermalHeadroom` (API 31, 10 s forecast) once a second and registers a thermal status listener (API 30), both by `dlsym`. `frame_stats` gains `thermal_h`, `thermal_status` and `thermal_level`. The start line lists the system's headroom thresholds per status (API 35).
  - Governor, off by default: `GRID_THERMAL_GOV=scale,cap` (each level adds the next step). `scale` lowers the dynamic render height's ceiling to 0.8 (`RenderScale::thermal_share`); `cap` lowers the frame cap to 30 (`android_display::Display::set_thermal_cap`, the setting and display hint unchanged). Hot: headroom ≥ `GRID_THERMAL_HOT` (0.8) or status ≥ MODERATE. Hysteresis: at most one raise per 5 s, one step down after 30 s cool (≤ `GRID_THERMAL_COOL`, 0.6). Three unit tests (config parsing, raise/fall timing, status-only and NaN headroom).
  - Phone soak of a static 30 cap against 60 (phone executor, 15 minutes, lean): race average 28.6 FPS against 25.9. After minute 2, 66 % of seconds reach 29+ FPS, against 6 %. Frames ≥ 50 ms: 1.2 % against 7.3 %. The heaviest minute still reaches GPU 29.5 ms and main 33.1 ms. This justifies the governor's `cap` step. Heavy views still need 15–20 % less CPU work under heat.
  - `GRID_ADPF_TARGET_MS` sets the target of the `adpf` session in `adpf.rs` (default 16.67 ms; 33.3 under a 30 cap, so the hint does not ask for 60 FPS clocks). A unit test covers the parsing.
  - The candidate table rates cool frame time and the throttled steady state separately. The expected ranking is written down before the phone numbers and marked as such.
- Vendored bevy_render patch (DRIG patch F935, third_party/README.md): `GRID_BEVY_PARALLEL_FINISH=1` defers each render system's `CommandEncoder::finish` from its serial apply to `PendingCommandBuffers::take`, which finishes them on the compute pool and keeps the push order. Why: on the phone (APK 50f84fe0) the 2D camera's first encode segment, 4.24 ms of the 9.78 ms `r_encode`, is the serial finish of the 3D camera's encoders; wgpu 29 records the Vulkan commands in `finish` (`encode_commands`), and different encoders finish independently (own lock plus a snatch read lock).
  - Test `crates/grid-app/tests/parallel_finish.rs` (ignored: needs a GPU; runs in the full tier): 24 items mixing deferred encoders, finished buffers and trailing `push_encoder` encoders; each copies a shared word into its history slot and writes its own number, so the read-back history proves the submit order.
  - The coordinator should refresh the build seed (`tools/build_seed.ps1`): third_party changed.
- `mesh_stream_writes` in `frame_split`: `queue.write_buffer` calls per main frame from `mesh_stream::upload` (each stages its own copy in wgpu), to judge whether coalescing the uploads (1.67 ms on the phone) is worth it.
- Ornament re-bounding (coordinator question, 55–62 entities per frame, mostly `ornament/*`): not a bug. The 6.1 ms `lap_cb` window is a wall-clock window over parallel PostUpdate work, not the bounds cost.
