F981 Mac 82c88068 traces: spike causes and why 60 Hz pacing drops (2026-11-27, Claude Code).
- Input: the owner's Mac (M4, 120 Hz ProMotion) traces at 82c88068, Donington and Istanbul, 3 laps each, kept owner-private under the main checkout's local/mac-traces/2026-11-27-82c88068/. These are the first traces with the F964 pacer (`refresh_pacing=auto`). The owner's comparison with 1546e211: frames of 20 ms or more fell from 25.1 % to 14.0 % (Donington) and from 26.5 % to 6.1 % (Istanbul); the median is 60 FPS instead of 66.
- Analysis: new `tools/pace_spikes.py`, one pass over both logs. It prints each pace change with the three windows before it, per-pace medians, and the top spike windows with the worst render frame's split and the events logged between windows. Output: local/mac-traces-82c88068/spikes.txt; a side check of dynamic-resolution moves: local/mac-traces-82c88068/dynres.py.
- Observed, per race window (one second):
  - Paced at 60: median acquire 0.04-0.05 ms, interval p90 17.0-17.5 ms, frames of 20 ms or more 3.7-3.9 %. At plain vsync: acquire 9.3-10.9 ms, interval p90 20 ms, 23.0-32.4 %. Main thread 3.7-4.6 ms, render CPU 5-6 ms and GPU 1.9-2.4 ms in both modes.
  - Donington spent 153 race windows paced and 153 at vsync, with 3 drops from 60 to vsync. Istanbul spent 279 and 26, with 1 drop (the other vsync windows are at the race start).
  - In the 2-3 windows before every drop from 60, the acquire (drawable) wait comes back to 2-11 ms per frame while main, render CPU and GPU stay unchanged. The pacer's `work_p90_ms` then reads 18.9-27.4 ms, above 60 Hz × `OVERRUN` (18.3 ms).
  - Spikes of 30 ms or more: most of them (11 of the top 16 windows) are acquire waits of 15-28 ms within a worst render frame of 20-34 ms, with nothing in common in the events (no camera change, loading, pipeline creation or image upload). The two largest after 6 s are different. Donington window 104 (48.1 ms, at vsync): render prepare 19.4 + encode 13.6 + acquire 7.9 ms. Istanbul window 42 (84.6 ms, paced): encode 70.8 ms. In both windows the main thread's longest fixed update is 10.8 and 12.6 ms (median about 2), and a `transform_burst` (about 2100 driver/body transforms) is logged. There is no `pipeline_new`, loading, texture or camera event in either window.
  - Dynamic resolution moved 57 times (Donington) and 40 times (Istanbul) between 1224 and 1440 rows. Its "late" moves measure frame_ms 17.6-18.4 ms while gpu_late_ms is 0-0.8 ms. Windows following a move have no more spikes of 30 ms or more than other windows.
- Interpretation:
  - Pacing drops because `work` (frame interval minus the pacing sleep, refresh_pace.rs) still contains the drawable wait. The pacing sleep runs on its own clock, not locked to the display's refresh. When the phase between the two drifts, the present just misses a refresh and the next acquire waits most of a period. The pacer then reads presentation time as frame work, leaves 60 for vsync (where every frame waits about 10 ms), and the per-rate back-off keeps it there for 20, 40 and 80 s. That explains the Donington half.
  - The two isolated spikes are render-thread CPU stalls, prepare/encode. Within the same second the main thread also has a long fixed update, so both threads stalled together. That points to a system-level or shared stall rather than GPU work or a pipeline compile. This is not proven: a per-frame timeline of both threads would be needed to tell.
  - Dynamic resolution lowers rows on presentation waits even though the GPU is on time (the same counting as the pacer). It costs some resolution but is not a spike cause in this data.
- Proposed (not built; sent to the coordinator for approval), general for all platforms:
  - Subtract each frame's acquire wait from the pacer's work time, so a presentation phase slip does not count as frame work.
  - Re-anchor the pace deadline to the display when an acquire wait shows a missed refresh, so the paced present lands before the next refresh instead of drifting.
  - Optionally, use the same acquire-free measure for dynamic resolution's "late".
- Next: on approval, implement both pacer changes with unit tests (a phase-slip second must not leave 60). Then the owner re-measures on the Mac. The two CPU spikes need a per-frame two-thread capture if they persist.
